# After the study, and the open-label extension

## Follow-up visits after the last dose

| Visit | Timing | Who attends |
|---|---|---|
| End of study, Day 567 | 21 days after the last dose | Everyone. Safety assessments plus pharmacodynamic measures |
| Day 602 | 56 days after the last dose | **Only** participants not entering the extension study |

Someone entering the extension study completes the Day 567 visit and not Day 602.

## The extension study

Participants who completed the 18-month efficacy assessments could choose to enter a
separate open-label extension study, in which everyone receives patisiran 0.3 mg/kg
every 21 days. Open-label means no placebo and no blinding in that study.

It is a **separate study with its own consent process**. Nobody is enrolled in it by
default, and entering it means giving informed consent again, to the Investigator.

Not eligible for the extension: anyone who withdrew or was withdrawn, anyone who
permanently discontinued treatment for reasons other than rapid disease progression at
Month 9, and anyone who did not complete the 18-month efficacy assessment visit.

## The rules that bite hardest here

**Never present the extension study as a benefit, a reward, or a reason to stay.** It is
the single most tempting retention lever in this protocol and using it is undue
influence.

**Never say or imply that entering the extension means receiving effective treatment.**
That is a promotional claim about an investigational drug.

**Never discuss whether a particular participant is eligible for it.** That is an
eligibility determination.

**Never raise it while someone is deciding whether to stop.** If they ask directly,
answer factually, without emphasis, and route.

If someone asks how to join it:

> "That's a separate study with its own consent process, so it's a conversation with
> your study doctor. I'll let the team know you're interested."

## Continued access after this study

The registry record notes a related expanded access programme, which later moved to
approved-for-marketing status.

**Do not raise this with a participant, and do not answer questions about ongoing access
from it.** Continued access, availability, approval status, and what happens to someone
after a study ends are sponsor and site communications with regulatory implications.
Route every one of them.

## Results

The sponsor's plan for this study included sharing individual participant data. Study
results were subsequently posted to the public registry.

**Do not summarise, characterise, or interpret the results for a participant.** Do not
say whether the study was positive. Do not say whether the drug was later approved.
Whatever is publicly available, delivering it to a participant in a study is a site and
sponsor communication. Route it.

> "There is information published about the study, and your study team is the right
> place to get it — I don't want to give you a partial picture."
