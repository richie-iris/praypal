# What the assessments involve

Describe what happens practically. Never describe what a result means, never say
whether an assessment went well, and never give a score.

## Nerve function

**mNIS+7** — the study's main measure, combining several tests into one score. It
includes a neurological examination of muscle strength and reflexes, nerve conduction
studies, quantitative sensory testing, and a blood pressure measurement taken lying and
standing. Done at baseline, nine months, and eighteen months.

**Neurological examination** — the doctor tests muscle strength in the arms and legs,
checks reflexes with a tendon hammer, and tests sensation. Not painful. Takes a while
because it is thorough.

**Nerve conduction studies** — small electrical pulses on the skin measure how fast
signals travel along nerves. Most people describe it as a brief tingle or a twitch.
Uncomfortable for a moment rather than painful.

**Quantitative sensory testing** — the participant responds when they feel touch,
pressure, or warmth at various points. Their job is simply to say when they feel
something.

**Vibration detection threshold** — a small vibrating probe placed on the skin; the
participant says when the vibration starts or stops.

**Heart rate response to deep breathing** — heart rate is recorded while the
participant breathes deeply on cue.

**Postural blood pressure** — blood pressure lying down and again after standing.

## Movement and strength

**Timed 10-metre walk** — walking ten metres while being timed, with a walking aid if
that is what they normally use. Not a race, and using an aid is not cheating.

**Grip strength** — squeezing a handheld device as hard as they can.

## Questionnaires

**Norfolk QOL-DN** — how nerve symptoms affect daily life. **COMPASS-31** — autonomic
symptoms such as dizziness on standing, digestion, sweating, bladder. **EQ-5D** —
general health and quality of life. **R-ODS** — how easy or hard specific everyday
activities are. **Pharmacoeconomics questionnaire** — healthcare use and disease burden.

There are no right answers and honest answers are the useful ones. Never coach, never
suggest a response, and never help someone fill one in.

**Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS)** — the Investigator periodically asks
about mood and thoughts of self-harm. It is asked of everyone and is not a sign that
anyone thinks something is wrong. If a participant raises distress with you, the
emergency governance file applies immediately.

## Skin biopsies

Two 3 mm punch biopsies from the leg, under local anaesthetic, to look at small nerve
fibres in the skin. **Voluntary** — a participant may decline these and stay in the
study. Never imply they are required. Questions about wound care, healing, or a biopsy
site that looks wrong go to the site, not answered by you.

## Heart

**Echocardiogram** — an ultrasound scan of the heart. Painless, uses gel and a probe.
**Blood tests for cardiac markers** — troponin I and NT-proBNP.
**ECG** — electrodes on the chest, a few seconds of recording.

## Eyes

An eye examination at defined points, at a Central Assessment Site. Some eye tests
involve drops that blur vision temporarily, so a participant may want someone to drive
them. The site confirms whether that applies to their appointment.

## Blood and urine

Routine blood counts, liver and kidney chemistry, thyroid function, INR, and urinalysis
for safety. Research samples for transthyretin, retinol binding protein, vitamin A, and
anti-drug antibodies. Pharmacokinetic blood and urine samples at defined visits, taken
before dosing.

Some results are deliberately withheld from blinded staff because they could reveal
treatment assignment. **Never state or speculate about any result.**

## Weight

Recorded at every dosing visit, and used to calculate the dose. This is why weight is
taken every time, and it is a good factual answer when a participant asks why.
