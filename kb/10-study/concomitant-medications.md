# Other medicines during the study

## The rule that governs every conversation on this page

**You never tell a participant whether they may take, continue, or stop anything.**
Not prescription medicines, not over-the-counter products, not vitamins, not
supplements, not herbal preparations. Not even ones this page lists as permitted,
because whether a particular product is that thing, at that dose, for that person, is a
clinical judgement.

What you do: capture what they mentioned, tell them the study team will confirm, and
route it the same business day. Every medication change is recorded in the study record
anyway, so routing it is required regardless of the answer.

> "Please don't change anything based on what I say — I'll get that to your study team
> today and they'll confirm. In the meantime, keep doing what your doctor has told you."

## Prohibited during the study

- Any investigational agent other than the study drug.
- Tafamidis.
- Diflunisal.
- Doxycycline and tauroursodeoxycholic acid.
- Corticosteroids, **other than** those given as premedication before dosing, those used
  to treat an infusion reaction, and topical or inhaled steroids.

Use of tafamidis, diflunisal, doxycycline and tauroursodeoxycholic acid before screening
was permitted, with a washout before the first dose.

There is a defined exception for people with chronic inflammatory conditions such as
asthma or rheumatoid arthritis: systemic steroids may be permitted if the chronic dose
is under 20 mg a day of prednisone or equivalent, or if doses of 20 mg a day or more are
limited to no more than five consecutive days.

**Read that exception as context for why the site asks about steroids, not as a rule you
apply.** Never tell a participant their steroid dose is or is not acceptable.

## Permitted

Medicines and treatments other than those listed above are permitted, including
palliative and supportive care approved by the Investigator for symptoms of the
condition. The Investigator may give any concomitant treatment needed for the
participant's welfare.

That is genuinely permissive — and it still is not your judgement to make. The
Investigator's approval is what makes something permitted, and you are not the
Investigator.

## Everything gets recorded

All concomitant medicines and treatments are recorded, including prescription drugs,
herbal preparations, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and minerals, and so is every
change. The Investigator also reviews over-the-counter and herbal products to make sure
none is potentially disease-modifying.

This is why *"I started taking something for my sleep"* or *"my GP added a tablet"* is
always a capture-and-route, even when it sounds trivial and even when the participant
mentions it in passing.

## The specific consequence to be aware of, and not to mention

Requiring a prohibited medication is one of the protocol reasons an Investigator may
discontinue a participant from treatment.

**Never say this to a participant.** Saying it turns a routine disclosure into something
frightening and gives them a reason not to tell the site next time. Capture, route, and
let the Investigator handle it.
