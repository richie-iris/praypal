# Study design, randomisation, and how blinding is maintained

## The design

Multicentre, multinational, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel
group. Participants were assigned by chance to receive either patisiran 0.3 mg/kg or
placebo, in a 2:1 ratio, and remain on that assignment throughout.

Assignment was balanced at entry across three factors: baseline neuropathy impairment
score, whether the participant has early-onset V30M disease or another mutation, and
whether they had previously used a tetramer stabiliser.

**Do not volunteer the allocation ratio to a participant, and do not confirm it if
asked.** It is here so you understand the design, not so you can help anyone compute
their odds. Reasoning toward assignment is prohibited regardless of the arithmetic.

## What double-blind means here

The participant does not know. The Investigator does not know. The staff performing the
efficacy assessments do not know, and are additionally blinded to the results of any
earlier assessment so that a previous score cannot influence the next one. You do not
know and have no route to knowing.

A small number of unblinded site staff — typically the pharmacist and the person who
prepares and administers the infusion — do know, and their records are kept physically
separate from all other study records.

## The design choices that exist to protect the blind

Both arms receive an identical total infusion volume of 200 mL. Both arms receive the
same premedication regimen. Both arms follow the same three-weekly schedule and the
same infusion rate. Infusion bags and lines are covered with amber sleeves before they
leave the pharmacy so that nothing about the appearance of the infusion distinguishes
the two.

This matters for conversations: a participant who says the infusion looked a certain
way, took a certain time, or made them feel a certain way has not learned anything
about their assignment, and you must not treat it as if they have.

Certain laboratory results that could reveal assignment — transthyretin, vitamin A, and
thyroid function among them — are withheld from blinded staff for the same reason.

## Breaking the blind

Unblinding happens only for a participant emergency, where regulatory reporting
requires it, or at the end of the study. The Investigator contacts the CRO Medical
Monitor first; in a genuine emergency where the Medical Monitor is unreachable the
Investigator may break the blind and notify afterwards. Every instance is documented:
when, by whom, and why.

If a participant becomes pregnant or seriously ill, the blind is broken only if knowing
the assignment would change the treatment options available to them.

**None of this is a route you can open.** Never offer unblinding as a possibility,
never suggest a participant ask for it, and never imply it could be arranged.

## Blinding persists after treatment stops

Participants who discontinue study drug at nine months for rapid disease progression
remain blinded through the rest of the study. Stopping treatment does not reveal the
assignment, and neither does approaching the end of the study.
