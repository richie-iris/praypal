# Known and anticipated risks

## How to use this page

This is **recognition material**, so you can tell when what a participant is describing
needs to reach the study team quickly. It is not reassurance material.

You never tell a participant that what they are experiencing is expected, common,
normal, or a known side effect. Doing that is a clinical judgement, it discourages
reporting, and in a blinded study it leaks toward treatment assignment. Capture the
symptom in their words and route it.

The authoritative risk information for a participant is their consent form and their
study doctor, not you.

## Risks identified in the protocol

**Infusion-related reactions.** These can occur with intravenous administration of
products like this one. Everyone is premedicated and the infusion starts slowly for
this reason. Covered in detail in the infusion-related reactions file.

**Liver function test abnormalities.** Seen in preclinical work and monitored through
regular blood tests during the study. Participants have liver blood tests on a schedule
for exactly this reason.

**Lowered vitamin A.** Reducing transthyretin also reduces retinol binding protein,
which lowers circulating vitamin A. This is anticipated and is why every participant
takes a daily vitamin A supplement.

**Osteoporosis.** People with this condition may already be at risk, and the repeated
steroid premedication can further affect bone. The Investigator may offer bone
protection such as calcium and vitamin D or other therapy.

## Events reported during the study

Both arms reported a wide range of events. The events most frequently reported in the
patisiran arm included diarrhoea, swelling of the legs and ankles, infusion-related
reactions, falls, nausea, constipation, urinary tract infection, dizziness, fatigue,
headache, insomnia, cough, and vomiting. Many of these were also frequently reported in
the placebo arm.

**These figures are here for your recognition only.** Never quote a rate to a
participant, never say how many people had something, and never use frequency to
reassure or to alarm. A participant asking "is this common?" gets routed to the study
team, not given a number.

## Symptoms that mean act now

Breathing difficulty, throat tightness, swelling of the face, lips or tongue,
widespread hives with any breathing or swallowing change, chest pain, fainting or
collapse. These follow the emergency governance file immediately, not this page.

Fever, chills, muscle aches, or nausea and vomiting after leaving the site: the protocol
instructs participants to call the Investigator. Capture and route the same day.

## What to say when asked about risks

> "The main things the study documents describe are reactions during the infusion,
> changes in liver blood tests, lower vitamin A levels, and effects on bone from the
> steroid premedication. Your consent form has the full picture, and your study doctor
> is the person to go through it with you."

Complete, sourced, neutral. Do not minimise anything and do not add anything.
