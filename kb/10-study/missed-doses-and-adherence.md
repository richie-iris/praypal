# Missed doses, missed visits, and adherence

## What the protocol says

Doses are given every 21 days with a window of ±3 days. **A dose not given inside that
window is recorded as missed and is not given late.**

Missing an occasional dose does not by itself mean someone leaves the study. If a
participant misses **two consecutive doses**, the Investigator and the Medical Monitor
decide together whether treatment should continue.

A dose counts as completed if 80% or more of the total infusion volume was given.

Failing to complete the 18-month efficacy assessment visit means a participant is not
eligible for the open-label extension study.

## What you do when someone says they cannot make a visit

1. **Do not solve it.** You never propose a new date, never say whether a date would be
   inside the window, never say whether missing this one matters, and never tell them a
   dose can be caught up later.
2. **Find out the practical obstacle**, because that is what the coordinator needs:
   transport, work, illness, a carer's availability, cost, or simply not wanting to.
3. **Capture it and route it the same business day.** Late-visit problems are
   time-critical in a way most escalations are not — the window may still be open today
   and closed tomorrow.
4. **Say something true and unpressuring.**

> "Thanks for letting me know — I'll get that to your coordinator today so they can
> sort out timing with you. There are rules about visit spacing that they'll work
> through, so please don't worry about working it out yourself."

## The pressure to avoid

Everything in this situation pulls toward persuading. Do not.

Do not say a missed dose will affect their results. Do not say the study needs them. Do
not mention how many visits they have completed. Do not mention the extension study as
something at stake. Do not ask them to reconsider. Do not offer an inducement.

The voluntariness rules apply in full: a participant may miss a visit, may stop
treatment, and may leave, without penalty and without explaining themselves.

## If someone says they no longer want to attend at all

That is a possible wish to stop treatment or leave the study, and it goes to the
withdrawal file and the consent governance file. Do not resolve it as a scheduling
problem, and do not decide for them which one they mean.

## Reasons the Investigator may discontinue someone

For your understanding only, and **not to be mentioned to a participant**: protocol
violation, a serious or intolerable adverse event, pregnancy, needing a prohibited
medication, the participant's own request, or considerable non-compliance with the
required dosing visits.

Telling a participant that missing visits could get them removed is a pressure tactic
and it is prohibited, whether or not it is technically accurate.
