# What this study is, in plain language

## The one-paragraph version

This study is testing an investigational medicine called patisiran in people who have
hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis with polyneuropathy — often called FAP.
Some participants receive patisiran and some receive a placebo, which is plain salt
water. Neither the participant nor the study team knows which. Everyone has an infusion
into a vein every three weeks for about eighteen months, and nerve function is measured
carefully along the way. The purpose is to find out whether patisiran slows the nerve
damage this condition causes. That question is not yet answered — answering it is the
whole reason the study exists.

## How to pitch it in a sentence on a call

> "It's a study testing whether an investigational medicine can slow the nerve damage
> caused by this condition, compared with a placebo."

Say what it is testing, never what it achieves. The promotion rules apply to every
description you give.

## The main things being measured

The primary measure is a nerve-function score called the mNIS+7, compared between the
two groups after eighteen months. Alongside it, the study measures quality of life, the
strength of specific muscles, nutritional status, walking speed over ten metres, and
symptoms of the autonomic nervous system.

For a participant, the useful framing is simply: *the study is measuring how your
nerves are working over time, in several different ways, and comparing the two groups.*

## What is asked of a participant

Roughly 21 months in total. A screening period of up to six weeks with several visits.
An infusion every three weeks for 78 weeks. Two long assessment visits, at about nine
months and about eighteen months, where no infusion is given and nerve testing takes
most of the day. Follow-up visits after the last dose. A daily vitamin A supplement
throughout. Contraception requirements for anyone who could become pregnant or father a
child.

## What patisiran is, if asked

It is an RNA interference therapeutic. In ordinary language: it is designed to reduce
the amount of a protein called transthyretin that the liver makes. In this condition,
that protein misfolds and builds up in nerves and other tissue. The medicine is given
into a vein because of how it is formulated.

Explain the mechanism if asked. Do not extend the explanation into a claim that
reducing the protein will help this participant. The step from mechanism to benefit is
exactly what the study is testing and exactly what you may not assert.
