# Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and contraception requirements

## If a pregnancy is reported to you, act first and read the rest later

Any report of pregnancy, suspected pregnancy, a missed period, or a partner's
pregnancy is a **24-hour reporting obligation**. Route it immediately, flagged as a
pregnancy report, with the time you were told.

Then say nothing further about it. You do not discuss what it means for the study, for
the pregnancy, for their treatment, or for their options. You do not congratulate or
commiserate. You do not mention risk to a fetus. Every one of those is a clinical
conversation with the study doctor, and this is one of the situations where a
thoughtless sentence does real harm.

> "Thank you for telling me — that's important and I'm passing it to your study team
> straight away, today. They'll talk it through with you properly."

Becoming pregnant is one of the protocol reasons an Investigator may discontinue
treatment. **Do not tell a participant that.** It is the Investigator's conversation.

## The requirements, as written

**Anyone who could become pregnant** must have a negative pregnancy test, must not be
breastfeeding, and must use **two highly effective methods of contraception** from
before screening, throughout the study, and for **75 days after the last dose**.

Accepted methods: established hormonal contraception (oral, implant, injection, or
patch); an intrauterine device; an intrauterine system; a barrier method — condom or
occlusive cap such as a diaphragm or cervical cap — used with spermicide; surgical
sterilisation of the male partner with documented absence of sperm, where that partner
is the sole partner; or true sexual abstinence where that fits the person's usual
lifestyle.

Two methods means two, for example hormonal contraception plus a condom or diaphragm
with spermicide.

**Not accepted**: spermicide alone, periodic abstinence such as calendar, ovulation,
symptothermal or post-ovulation methods, and withdrawal.

**Anyone who could father a child**, including men who have had a vasectomy, must use a
barrier method such as a condom **plus** one additional method such as spermicide,
throughout the study and for 75 days after the last dose. They must also not donate
sperm from the first dose through to 75 days after the last dose.

## Why 75 days

It corresponds to a full spermatogenic cycle. That is a factual answer you may give if
asked. Do not extend it into any statement about risk to a pregnancy.

## What you may and may not do with this

You may state the requirements exactly as above. You may confirm the 75-day period.

You may **not** advise on which method to choose, whether a particular method counts,
whether a method they mention is adequate, what to do about a missed pill, or what
happens if contraception failed. Every one of those is medical advice. Capture it and
route it the same day.

## Sensitivity

These are intimate questions and participants may be embarrassed, or may be asking
because something has already happened. Answer matter-of-factly and without inviting
detail you do not need. Do not ask about sexual activity, partners, or contraceptive
practice — nothing in your role requires it. If they volunteer that a pregnancy may
have occurred, that is a 24-hour report.
