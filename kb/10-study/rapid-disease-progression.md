# The rapid disease progression pathway at nine months

## What it is

The study includes a defined safeguard at the nine-month point. An independent clinical
adjudication committee reviews, while blinded, whether a participant's disease has
progressed rapidly against a pre-specified definition. Where it has, the participant's
treating physician offers them the **choice** of stopping study drug and receiving local
standard of care for their condition instead.

The threshold is defined in the protocol as an increase of 24 points or more in the
mNIS+7 score from baseline, based on an average of two measurements, together with
progression in disease stage.

**Never apply that definition to anyone, never estimate whether someone meets it, and
never state or imply anything about a particular participant's scores.** The
adjudication committee decides; the treating physician has the conversation.

## Why it exists — the framing that helps

This pathway is a protection built into the study. It means someone whose disease is
progressing quickly is not left without options while the trial continues.

That is a fair and accurate thing to say if a participant asks what it is for. It is not
an invitation to speculate about whether it will apply to them.

## What happens to someone on this pathway

They keep their choice about study drug, in discussion with the Investigator on Day 273,
which has a window of +3 days only. If they stop study drug they may receive other
treatment for their condition per local standard of care.

They **remain in the study** and follow a modified visit schedule through the 18-month
efficacy assessments. **Blinding is maintained throughout** — stopping study drug on this
pathway does not reveal their assignment.

They may still be eligible for the open-label extension study if they complete the
18-month assessments.

Anyone on this pathway who also decides to leave the study has no further visits after
the Day 273 assessments and is not eligible for the extension study.

## The modified schedule

Different visits from the standard schedule, including **telephone contacts** at
protocol-specified points where study personnel ask about general health status and any
treatments received for the condition. This is the protocol's own provision for phone
follow-up and it is the closest the study comes to describing what you do.

Adverse events and concomitant medications are collected through Day 294 for these
participants. After Day 294 only study-procedure-related events are collected, such as
those related to a skin biopsy or a blood draw. Serious adverse events continue to be
followed by the Investigator.

**That narrowing does not narrow what you capture.** You are not the filter. Capture
everything a participant reports and let the site apply the protocol rules.

## What you say when someone asks about it

> "There's a review at the nine-month point where an independent committee looks at how
> people are doing. If it applies to you, your study doctor will talk it through with you
> directly and you'd have a choice about what happens next."

Then stop. Do not estimate whether it applies to them, do not comment on how they seem
to be doing, and do not connect anything they have told you to it.
