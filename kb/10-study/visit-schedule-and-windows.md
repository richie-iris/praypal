# Visit schedule, windows, and what happens when

## Overall shape

About 21 months from screening to final follow-up. Up to 42 days of screening, 78 weeks
of dosing every three weeks, and follow-up visits after the last dose.

| Stage | Timing | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Screening | Days −42 to 0, over three or more visits | Consent first, then eligibility checks, medical history, baseline nerve and heart assessments |
| Day 0 | Baseline | First infusion |
| Routine dosing visits | Day 21 through Day 546, every 21 days | Premedication, checks, infusion, one hour observation |
| 9-month efficacy assessment | Days 253 to 272 | Full nerve-function testing. **No infusion on these days** |
| Day 273 | +3 days only | Review of the 9-month results with the Investigator |
| 18-month efficacy assessment | Days 553 to 560 | Full nerve-function testing. **No infusion on these days** |
| End of study | Day 567 | 21 days after last dose; safety assessments and pharmacodynamic measures |
| Second follow-up | Day 602 | 56 days after last dose. **Only for participants not entering the extension study** |

## Windows — the rule participants ask about most

All visits carry a window of **±3 days**, with one exception: **Day 273 has a window of
+3 days only**. It cannot move earlier, because the nine-month nerve testing has to be
complete before that discussion happens.

A dose not given within its window is recorded as missed and is not administered late.

**You never approve, decline, or advise on moving a visit.** Rescheduling interacts with
the whole downstream schedule and it is a site decision. State the window if asked,
then route the request to the coordinator the same day.

## Two site types

Some participants attend two different places. **Central Assessment Sites** perform the
specialised efficacy testing at screening, baseline, nine months, and eighteen months.
**Patient Care Sites** handle screening, dosing, and day-to-day management, and send
participants to the nearest Central Assessment Site for those four assessment points.

So a participant may legitimately have a different location for their nine-month and
eighteen-month visits. Confirm the location from their record rather than assuming it
is the usual site.

## What is added on particular days

Routine visits always include weight, vital signs, concomitant medication review, and
adverse event review before dosing. On top of that, specific days carry extra
assessments:

- **Physical examination**: Days 252 and 546.
- **12-lead ECG**: Days 126 and 399.
- **Transthyretin, retinol binding protein, vitamin A**: Days 21, 126, 252, 273, 399, 546.
- **Serum chemistry**: Days 84, 189, 357, 462, 546.
- **Haematology, thyroid function, urinalysis**: Days 252 and 546.
- **Anti-drug antibodies**: Days 21, 126, 252, 399, 546.
- **Pharmacokinetic blood and urine samples**: Days 21, 126, 252, 399, 546, before dosing.
- **INR**: Day 546.
- **Eye examination**: Day 21 if not already done, once between Days 231 and 272, and
  once between Days 546 and 560, at a Central Assessment Site.

Use this to answer "will I need bloods this time?" — but always tell the participant the
site will confirm the specifics for their visit.

## Efficacy assessment visits are long

The nine-month and eighteen-month visits involve extensive nerve testing and take
substantially longer than a dosing visit. **No study drug is given on those days.**
Participants who expect an infusion should be told there isn't one, and that this is
normal and planned.

## Follow-up after the last dose

Participants entering the open-label extension study complete only the Day 567 visit.
Participants not entering it complete both Day 567 and Day 602.

## Unscheduled visits

The protocol allows visits outside the schedule when clinically needed. Never tell a
participant to come in or not to come in. If they think they need to be seen, route it
to the coordinator, and if it is urgent, apply the emergency rules.
