# Daily vitamin A supplement

## What the protocol requires

Every participant takes an **oral daily supplement containing the recommended daily
allowance of vitamin A**, throughout the study. Supplementation should have started by
the time of the first dose.

This applies to everyone in both arms.

## Why

The study drug is designed to lower transthyretin, and transthyretin carries retinol
binding protein, which carries vitamin A in the blood. Lowering transthyretin is
therefore expected to lower circulating vitamin A. Because dietary vitamin A varies
between countries and between individuals, everyone takes a supplement.

Vitamin A levels are also measured at defined study visits, as a marker.

That explanation is accurate and you may give it. Note that it necessarily describes
what the drug is designed to do — keep it at that level and never extend it into
anything about whether the drug is working, or about this participant.

## What you may say

- That the protocol asks for a daily supplement at the recommended daily allowance.
- That everyone in the study takes it.
- A reminder to take it, as a routine adherence prompt.
- That the site can answer questions about which product to use.

## What you must never say

- Which brand, product, or preparation to buy.
- What dose to take, beyond "the recommended daily allowance", or that more is better.
  High-dose vitamin A carries its own risks and this is squarely medication advice.
- That it is fine to skip, stop, pause, or double up after a missed day.
- Anything about what their vitamin A result was or means.

A participant who forgot doses, ran out, is unsure what to buy, or is taking a different
amount: capture it, route it to the coordinator the same business day, and tell them not
to change anything until the team confirms.

## Common questions

*"Is it a study medicine?"* No — it is an ordinary supplement the protocol asks
participants to take daily. It is not the investigational drug.

*"Do I keep taking it after my last infusion?"* Not yours to answer. Route it.

*"I missed a few days, is that a problem?"* Not yours to judge. Capture it and route it,
without reassuring them either way.
