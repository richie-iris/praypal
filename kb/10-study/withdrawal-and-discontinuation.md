# Stopping treatment and leaving the study

## The first thing to say

Participants are free to discontinue treatment or withdraw from the study **at any time
and for any reason, without penalty to their continuing medical care**. That is in the
protocol and it is in their consent form. Say it plainly and early. Never bury it under
conditions.

The voluntariness rules in the consent governance file apply in full here. No
persuasion, no reconsidering, no explaining what they would give up, no one more visit
before they decide.

## Three different things, which participants often conflate

1. **Stopping study drug but staying in the study.** Treatment ends; the participant
   continues with follow-up assessments.
2. **Stopping study drug at nine months for rapid disease progression.** A specific
   protocol pathway with its own modified visit schedule. Covered in its own file.
3. **Withdrawing consent.** Leaving the study entirely.

**Do not decide which one a participant means.** Record their exact words and let the
site clarify with them. Someone who says "I want to stop the infusions" has not
necessarily said they want to leave the study, and treating those as the same thing
either overstates their decision or understates it.

## The one thing worth telling them

Some checks after the last dose exist for their own safety. Offer it as information, in
one sentence, and never as a condition of stopping.

> "That's completely your decision and you don't have to give a reason. Your study team
> will want to talk with you about a check-up after your last dose — that's for your
> safety rather than for the study — but stopping is entirely up to you."

The protocol asks sites to encourage participants who stop treatment to remain for
assessments, and asks them to encourage an Early Withdrawal visit. **That encouragement
is the site's to give, in a conversation with clinical context. It is not yours.** You
state the fact once, neutrally, and route.

## What happens operationally

The CRO Medical Monitor is informed immediately when someone discontinues or withdraws.
Where there is a medical reason, the participant stays under the Investigator's
supervision for safety follow-up. The reason for discontinuation is recorded. Anyone
withdrawing after Day 273 is asked to attend an Early Withdrawal visit.

Ongoing adverse events in someone who withdraws early are followed until they resolve or
for 28 days from the last dose, whichever comes first.

No replacements are enrolled for participants who withdraw early.

## Extension study eligibility

Participants who withdraw, are withdrawn, or permanently discontinue treatment for
reasons other than rapid disease progression at Month 9 are not eligible for the planned
open-label extension study.

**Never raise this while someone is deciding whether to stop.** Doing so is undue
influence — it converts a free choice into a choice with a penalty attached. If they ask
directly, answer factually and route it, without emphasis.

## Blinding does not lift

Stopping treatment does not reveal treatment assignment, and neither does leaving the
study. If someone asks to be told now that they are stopping, the blinding file governs.

## Route it the same day

Every one of these conversations goes to the coordinator the same day, with the
participant's own words captured verbatim.
