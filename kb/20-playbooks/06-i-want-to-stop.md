# Playbook — "I want to stop"

The playbook where the most damage gets done, because everything about a retention call
pulls toward persuading, and persuading here is a regulatory violation.

## The four moves, in order, with nothing added

**1. Accept it immediately.**
> "That's completely your decision, and you don't need to give a reason."

First sentence. Not after a question. Not after establishing why.

**2. Capture their exact words.**
Verbatim. Do not interpret which of the possible decisions they mean.

**3. Offer the one neutral safety fact, once.**
> "Your study team will want to talk with you about a check-up after your last dose —
> that's for your safety rather than for the study. But whether you carry on is entirely
> up to you."

Once, as information, never as a condition, never repeated.

**4. Route the same day** and tell them who will call and when.

## The list of things you must not say

Not gently, not once, not because it is true.

- "Are you sure?" / "Would you think about it?"
- "Can I ask why?" — asking for a reason implies one is owed.
- "You're so close to the end."
- "You'd be giving up the chance to join the extension study."
- "Your data really matters to the research."
- "Other people find it gets easier."
- "Could you do one more visit and then decide?"
- "Let me just take a few details first."
- Any mention of reimbursement, of what the site has invested, or of how far they have come.

If a reason is volunteered, record it. Do not ask for one.

## Do not decide what they meant

"I want to stop the infusions" is not necessarily "I want to leave the study." Do not
collapse them and do not ask a leading question to resolve it. Record what they said and
let the site clarify with them.

Similarly, "stop calling me" is a contact preference, never a study withdrawal.

## If a reason surfaces that you can act on

Sometimes the reason is practical — transport, cost, work, a carer. You may capture it
and route it as an obstacle **without** offering to fix it and without using it to
reopen the decision.

> "Thanks for telling me. I'll pass that on with everything else — but the decision
> stays yours either way."

Never: "if we sorted the transport, would you stay?" That is a bargain, and offering one
is undue influence.

## If they are distressed

Stay with them. Do not rush to end the call. If there is hopelessness or self-harm, the
emergency governance file takes over immediately.

## Close

Confirm what you have recorded, tell them who will be in touch, thank them plainly. Do
not end on anything that sounds like a last appeal.
