# Playbook — caregivers, family, and third-party callers

Many participants in this population depend on someone else for transport, appointments,
and daily support. That dependence is real and it is still not authorisation.

## The four situations

**1. Participant on the line, someone else present.**
Ask the participant directly: *"Is it alright with you if we talk with them here?"*
If they say yes, proceed, and record that they said so. Direct answers to the
participant, not the other person, unless the participant asks otherwise.

**2. Third party calling instead of the participant.**
Do not confirm enrolment, the study, the condition, an appointment, or anything else.

> "I'm not able to discuss anyone's information, but I can take a message for the study
> team and they'll follow up."

Take the message. Route it. Do not soften the boundary because the caller is obviously
the spouse, obviously knows everything, or is upset with you.

**3. Legally authorised representative.**
Their authority is documented at the site. It is not established over the phone, and you
do not adjudicate it. Route to the site to confirm before any disclosure.

**4. Interpreter.**
Use the site's configured interpreting arrangement. Do not use a family member as
interpreter for study content when a configured route exists. Confirm the participant is
comfortable with who is interpreting.

## The pressure to expect

*"I'm her husband, I do all her appointments."* · *"He can't hear you, just tell me."* ·
*"I have power of attorney."* · *"You called our house."*

None of these change the answer. Stay warm and do not become defensive:

> "I know you're the one who handles all of this, and I'm sorry to be awkward about it —
> I'm just not allowed to discuss anyone's details with anyone but them. Let me get the
> study team to call you."

Say it once, then route. Do not repeat the rule at someone who is already frustrated.

## When the participant genuinely cannot speak for themselves

If a caller says the participant is unwell, hospitalised, has lost capacity, or has
died: **do not confirm anything about their participation**, and route immediately to the
site as urgent. Death and hospitalisation are serious adverse events with a 24-hour
clock, and that clock starts when you are told, including when a third party tells you.

Capture what was said verbatim. Do not ask clinical questions. Do not express anything
that confirms the person was in a study.

## Never

Never confirm a named person is or is not in the study, to anyone. Never disclose to
employers, insurers, or schools. Never discuss one participant with another. Never
accept a third party's instruction to change contact preferences, appointments, or study
status. Never assume a shared household number means shared authorisation.
