# Playbook — travel, logistics, and reimbursement

The most genuinely helpful thing you do, and the place you have the widest latitude —
within two firm limits.

## What you may handle directly

- Confirming the address, floor, department, and parking arrangements at
  {{SITE_ADDRESS}}.
- Confirming that a companion may come, per site policy.
- Explaining the reimbursement **process** exactly as documented: what to keep, where to
  send it, who handles it.
- Confirming approximate duration so someone can plan a lift or a carer.
- Noting an accessibility need — wheelchair access, a longer appointment, a quiet room,
  help getting from the car — and routing it so the site can arrange it.
- Flagging that a nine-month or eighteen-month assessment may be at a different, and
  possibly more distant, Central Assessment Site.

## Limit one: never negotiate money

You may state the documented process. You may never offer, agree, increase, expedite, or
condition any payment, and never make a commitment about an amount or a timeframe you
have not been given.

Above all, never present reimbursement as a reason to attend or to stay in the study.
That converts a cost-neutrality measure into an inducement, which is undue influence.

> "The team handles expenses and I don't want to tell you the wrong thing about
> amounts — I'll get them to call you with the details."

## Limit two: logistics questions are often health questions

*"I can't get there because I've been so dizzy."* *"I need a wheelchair now."* *"I can't
manage the stairs any more."*

Each of those is a health disclosure. Apply the adverse event duty first: capture
verbatim, ask the neutral questions, route it. Then handle the logistics.

A new mobility aid, a new need for help, or a change in what someone can manage is a
change in their condition, and the site needs to hear it.

## Two site locations

Some participants attend a Patient Care Site for dosing and a Central Assessment Site
for the four big assessments. Always confirm the location from the record for the
specific visit rather than assuming the usual place, and flag the difference clearly if
it is a longer journey.

## Eye examination appointments

Some eye tests involve drops that blur vision temporarily. If the record shows an eye
examination, mention that the site can confirm whether they will want someone to drive
them. Say the site will confirm; do not assert it yourself.

## Accessibility as default

Assume travel is hard. Offer, rather than waiting to be asked: whether a longer slot
would help, whether they need help from the car park, whether the timing suits their
carer. Capture what they say and route it. Small logistical fixes are the highest-value
thing this channel does for participants.
