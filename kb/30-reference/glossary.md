# Glossary — study terms in plain language

Plain-language equivalents for terms participants encounter. Use these to explain a
word. Do not use them to explain a result, a score, or what any of it means for a
particular person.

## Study conduct

**Adverse event** — any unwanted medical occurrence in someone taking part, whether or
not it is thought to be caused by the treatment.
**Serious adverse event** — one that is life-threatening, causes death, means a hospital
stay or a longer one, causes lasting disability, is a birth defect, or is medically
important enough to need intervention.
**Baseline** — measurements taken before treatment starts, used as the comparison point.
**Blinded / double-blind** — participants, investigators, and assessing staff do not know
who is receiving the drug and who is receiving placebo.
**Case report form** — the record where study data about each participant is entered.
**Discontinuation** — stopping study treatment. Not the same as leaving the study.
**Informed consent** — the documented process in which someone is given full information
and freely agrees to take part; they keep a signed copy.
**Investigational drug** — a medicine still being studied and not yet approved for this use.
**Investigator** — the doctor responsible for the study and for participants' care at a site.
**IRB / ethics committee** — the independent group that reviews and approves the study to
protect participants' rights and wellbeing.
**Open-label** — everyone knows what is being given; no placebo, no blinding.
**Placebo** — a preparation with no active medicine; here, sterile salt water.
**Protocol** — the document setting out exactly how the study is run.
**Protocol deviation** — something that did not happen as the protocol requires.
**Randomised** — assigned to a group by chance rather than by choice.
**Screening** — checks before the study starts, to see whether someone can take part.
**Sponsor** — the organisation responsible for the study; here, Alnylam Pharmaceuticals.
**Study coordinator** — the site staff member who is a participant's day-to-day contact.
**Washout** — a period off a medicine before starting a study.
**Withdrawal** — leaving the study.

## This study specifically

**ATTR / hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis** — an inherited condition in
which a misfolded protein builds up in tissues including the nerves and heart.
**FAP** — familial amyloidotic polyneuropathy; the nerve form of that condition.
**Transthyretin (TTR)** — the protein involved, made mainly by the liver.
**Patisiran (ALN-TTR02)** — the investigational medicine, designed to lower transthyretin.
**RNA interference / siRNA** — the approach used, reducing production of a specific protein.
**Lipid nanoparticle** — the tiny fatty package that delivers the medicine to the liver.
**Infusion-related reaction (IRR)** — a reaction during or shortly after an infusion.
**Premedication** — medicines given before the infusion to reduce the chance of a reaction.
**mNIS+7 / NIS** — combined scores measuring nerve function; the study's main measure.
**Nerve conduction study** — small electrical pulses measuring how fast nerve signals travel.
**Quantitative sensory testing** — measuring what someone can feel: touch, pressure, warmth.
**COMPASS-31** — questionnaire about autonomic symptoms such as dizziness and digestion.
**Norfolk QOL-DN** — questionnaire about how nerve symptoms affect daily life.
**EQ-5D** — general quality-of-life questionnaire.
**R-ODS** — questionnaire about how easy or hard everyday activities are.
**FAP stage / PND score** — scales describing how much walking and daily function are affected.
**Modified BMI (mBMI)** — a nutrition measure combining body mass index with a blood protein.
**Retinol binding protein (RBP)** — the protein that carries vitamin A; it travels with
transthyretin, which is why vitamin A is supplemented.
**Anti-drug antibodies** — antibodies the body may make against the study drug; measured
routinely.
**Pharmacokinetics** — how much of a drug is in the body and how it is cleared.
**Pharmacodynamics** — what the drug does in the body; here, transthyretin levels.
**Extension study** — a separate study, with its own consent, in which everyone receives
active drug.

## Terms to use with care

**Efficacy** — say "whether it works," and only ever as the open question.
**Compliance / adherence** — say "keeping to the visit schedule." Never use these words
to a participant; they sound like judgement.
**Subject** — say "participant," always, to and about people in the study.
